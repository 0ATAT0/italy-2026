Field notes

Heat. Sites 9:00–12:00, AC or sea 12:00–17:00, everything social after dark. Rome logic, hotter pavement.

Cash. €40–60 a day — market stalls, porters, beach kiosks, taxis with "broken" readers. Bancomat ATMs attached to banks.

Taxis. In Naples ask for the tariffa predeterminata — fixed posted fares — or insist on the meter. Positano: cars stop at Piazza dei Mulini; porters (~€6/bag, cash) do the rest.

Trains. The Circumvesuviana is the pickpocket capital of Campania. Bags front, phones zipped. Campania Express when the timetable fits.

Ferragosto week. Coast businesses close or change hours 15–25 Aug. Trust Instagram over Google hours. Emergency number 112; GHIC works in Italy.

A quarter Pompeii's size, twice the preservation — the surge carbonised wood instead of crushing it, so you walk two-storey houses with beams, doors, furniture. The Ferragosto exit

Italy's peak holiday date. Whichever option wins gets booked the day it's chosen.

The road is the trap: SS163 is a car park on Ferragosto and Positano runs its own procession. Any car plan needs a 3-hour budget and still carries the risk; the ferry's only enemy is a sell-out — which is why it books now.
A

Ferry + taxi decide

dep ~12:30 · port ~13:50 · NAP ~14:30~€31 pp + taxi

Lucibello ~12:30 (or NLG ~10:55) to Beverello, then 25–35 min to the airport — city-side traffic is normal; the chaos lives on the coast road. Lands ~5 hours before the flight: dull, bulletproof. Not the last sailings (~15:40/16:24): a cancellation there leaves no road fallback that makes 19:40.

B

Private van, whole group decide

leave 13:00–13:30 · budget 3 h+~€180–280 the vehicle

With more of you going back, one van door-to-door beats per-head tickets and swallows the luggage mountain. Leaving by 13:30 with a 3-hour budget arrives ~16:30 for 19:40 — workable, zero slack for a real jam. Pickup at the ZTL edge (Piazza dei Mulini); porter the bags down.

Split option: luggage + volunteers in the van, the impatient on the boat.

The call

Ferry fails into an afternoon lost at the airport; the van fails into a missed flight. Both clear the bar on paper. Needed: headcount, luggage count, the family's appetite for road risk — then book same day.

The tomato answer

You are sailing through the Odyssey

Good year for it. Everything below is walkable from, or visible from, this itinerary.

Naples is a Greek city

Founded ~470 BC as Neapolis — "new city" — by Greeks from Cumae, over the older settlement of Parthenope: the siren who drowned herself when Odysseus sailed past unharmed and washed ashore where the city rose. Neapolitans are still partenopei; so is the football club. The grid you walk on Friday night is the Greek grid — Spaccanapoli is the ancient decumanus inferior.

Touch it: the city walls exposed in Piazza Bellini, spritz in hand · the Greek agora under San Lorenzo Maggiore on the pizza street · the Greek rooms at MANN.

Positano faces the Sirens

The three islets off Spiaggia Grande are Li Galli — the Sirenusai, the canonical home of Homer's Sirens, stated as plain geography by Strabo two thousand years ago. The archipelago's older name is Le Sirenuse. At Punta Campanella, the peninsula's tip, legend holds Odysseus himself founded the temple of Athena after passing them tied to the mast.

Sail it: the sunset cruise circles the islands with a swim stop — as close to the Sirens as the law allows.

The sunset cruise

One eruption ties the trip together

Pompeii and Herculaneum on Saturday; their treasures at MANN that evening; and beneath Positano's own church, the MAR villa — buried by the same week of ash in 79 AD, eleven metres down, a glass walkway over its frescoes. Four sites, one catastrophe, one holiday.

Virgil holds both ends

He lived and wrote the Georgics in Naples, set Aeneas' descent to the underworld at Cumae just up the coast, and is buried at Piedigrotta under the epitaph: "Mantua bore me, Calabria took me, now Parthenope holds me."

And the philosophers, since you asked: Pythagoras taught at Croton, deep in Calabria — but the Eleatic school of Parmenides and Zeno sat at Elea, on the Cilento coast two hours south of your beach. That is as close as philosophy gets to the sun loungers.
